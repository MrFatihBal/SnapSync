The idea

Three actions on site. Everything else is automatic.

No forms to fill in, no system to choose, nothing to remember at the end of the day. Your station decides what the app shows you.

1

Scan

Point the camera at the barcode. The app reads it, checks it against the rules for your station and locks it in.

2

Photograph

Take the photos the station asks for. Each one is resized and compressed on the device before it ever touches the network.

3

Forget about it

The record is saved on the phone first. If there is no signal it waits in the queue and sends itself when you are back in coverage.

    Installation

    Getting it onto a device

    Android only. Takes about five minutes on a device you control.

    1

    Check the requirements

    An Android tablet or phone, an SGS Microsoft account, and a network that can reach the SnapSync API. Mobile data is fine.

    https://gkgm.sgs.com.tr/health
    2

    Download the APK

    Use the build link shared with you. Save the file somewhere safe — build artifacts expire, the installed app does not.

    3

    Allow the install

    Android will ask for permission to install from this source. Approve it for your browser or file manager, then open the APK again.

    Settings › Apps › Special access › Install unknown apps
    4

    Sign in and allow the camera

    Open the app, tap Microsoft ile Giriş Yap and sign in with your SGS account. Grant the camera permission when prompted — it is the only permission the app asks for.

    Managed devices

    If the tablet is enrolled in company device management, a sideloaded APK will be removed automatically and you will see “Yöneticiniz tarafından silindi”. On those devices the app has to be distributed through Intune as a line-of-business app, or the package com.sgs.snapsync has to be allow-listed. Ask IT.

    Updates

    Interface changes arrive on their own — close the app fully, reopen it, close it once more and reopen. Only changes to the app shell itself need a new APK.

    Good to know

    Questions that come up on site

    What happens when there is no signal?

    Nothing stops. The record is written to the device the moment you finish, and it sits in the queue. The app retries on its own every time you bring it to the foreground, so simply opening it back in coverage is enough. Automatic retries stop after five failures; manual retry always works.

    Where are the photos kept?

    In the app's own private storage, not the gallery. Once a photo is accepted by the server its local copy is deleted. Sent records are cleared after 24 hours; anything still unsent is kept until it goes through.

    How much mobile data does this use?

    Photos are resized to a maximum of 2500 px on the long edge and saved as JPEG at 0.8 quality. In practice that is a median of about 375 KB and an average of about 440 KB per photo — roughly 400 MB for a thousand photos.

    Can I choose a different station or project?

    No, and that is deliberate. The assignment comes from your SnapSync profile. If it is wrong, it has to be corrected on the server side — changing it on the device would risk filing photos into the wrong system.

    It says my station is not defined.

    Your assignment exists on the server but the app does not know it yet. This happens when a new system is opened before the app is updated. The screen shows your project, station and assignment number — send those to the development team and it is a one-line fix.

    Sign-in fails. What do I check?

    Read the reason in the error box — it distinguishes a certificate problem, a DNS problem and a blocked connection. First try https://gkgm.sgs.com.tr/health in the device browser. If that works but the app does not, send a screenshot of the error to the development team.

    Is there a diagnostics screen?

    Yes, hidden on purpose. On the home screen tap the logo five times within three seconds. It shows a live log, which app bundle is running, and a button to pull updates manually. It is meant for support, not for field staff.
